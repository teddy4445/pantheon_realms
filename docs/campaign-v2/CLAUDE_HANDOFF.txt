3. CAMPAIGN STORY AND CHAPTER STRUCTURE

Keep the established premise: six separate worlds collided through a mysterious event. The gods failed to agree; direct divine combat would unravel the damaged world. Existing mortal champions pursue their gods' claims through mythical armies, local alliances and conquest. Keep the collision's ultimate cause unresolved. Avoid inventing a universal seventh villain or turning the opening into an address to the player.

New playable count: tutorial 2; Greek 6; Norse 6; Egyptian 6; Japanese 6; Celtic 6; Mesoamerican 5 = 37. Existing protagonists: Alexios, Hervor, Seshet, Akane, Orla and Itzel. Alexios also leads the tutorial. All original 45 chapter story beats are preserved in the mapped 37 missions. Do not simply drop eight old chapters or rename old maps.

The authoritative per-mission text is design/mission-content.json. Implement every card's story, tasks, terrain connections, pressure, local choice and finish condition. The reference review gives campaign-wide supporting cast, continuity and narrative arcs. Keep map objectives observable and journaled, not only spoken in an introductory paragraph.

New runtime mission IDs are proposed as <campaign>_v2_mNN. Keep old IDs and definitions available for legacy saves. campaign-graph.json uses requiresAll: every listed predecessor is required. Two available chapters are an order choice, not mutually exclusive alternative endings. No campaign runs two chapter maps at once. The player completes one, carries bounded progress forward, then chooses the next available chapter.

4. MAPS MUST BECOME REGIONS, NOT CORRIDORS

The old repeated Restore/Hunt/Covenant/Seek/Rival/Refuge/Finale pipeline and its river/mountain strips are part of the problem. Do not solve it by rotating a strip or exchanging terrain textures. Author each region graph from its mission card before adding decorations or guards.

Beyond the first tutorial minutes, each main map needs a complete useful circuit, two plausible approaches to its principal objective, and a shortcut opened through play. Routes must differ in safety, distance, income, allies or information. Do not make every route a key gate followed by the same mandatory fight. Optional dead ends are short reward pockets. Some finales return to a changed home region rather than marching to the right edge.

Roads meet actual town gates, bridge landings, ferry approaches and site interaction cells. Forest placement follows its biome. The two sluice images are states of the same site; opening must change the relevant water/route rule and visual together. Use a common ground anchor and fixed display size; inspect alignment of the two separate paintings before shipping. The ferry is a route connector, not a promise of free movement over every water tile. Reuse the game's existing crossings/sites where sufficient.

Use finite, signalled enemy pressure. A patrol has a home and a purpose; taking its camp stops that source. Do not generate endless mandatory waves. Start a scenario timer only when its announced triggering phase begins, show the deadline before confirmation, and save the trigger day. Never delete essential evidence or introduce an invisible failure deadline. Make loss of an optional reward visibly different from mission defeat.

Pacing targets in the cards are unplaytested. Most middle maps target a handful of meaningful mandatory tactical encounters; finales can have more. Leave ordinary resource pickups unguarded when no interesting battle is gained. Test navigation with the actual fixed camera. Map input pans left/right/up/down; do not introduce camera rotation to compensate for bad placement.

6. SIX NEUTRAL CREATURES

All six are unaffiliated and recruitable by any pantheon at appropriate sites. “Neutral” does not mean an ordinary real-world animal: their mythology provides flavor, not faction ownership. Keep them out of the seven-tier town rosters. They are optional sidegrades; every mandatory campaign objective must remain achievable with the current creatures.

Pegasus: flying escort, high meadows/limestone uplands; folded-wing core and separate flight art.
Root-Runner: ground scout/distraction, wooded roots and mossy groves; Ratatoskr-inspired animal, not the named divine messenger.
Serpopard: long-reach predator, sandstone/river edges; no forest shrine on open dunes.
Baku: protective support, misty forest groves; nightmare-eater theme.
Afanc: slow defensive freshwater beast, lakes/reed pools; not open-ocean decoration.
Ahuitzotl: limited pull/ambush, warm freshwater cenotes and jungle banks; tail-hand must remain readable.

Use design/neutral-content.json for proposed rules and starter values. They are deliberately marked unplaytested. The audited rules have no fear status; Baku therefore cleanses a real temporary hostile debuff in the initial implementation. Keep the nightmare-cleansing lore, but do not ship a button whose stated fear-cleansing effect has no possible target. If a fear status is added later, extend the same eligibility predicate explicitly. Do not silently grant an unrelated old ability because its icon was reused.

These are six single-form species. There are no new upgrade portraits or upgrade units. The current CreatureFamilyDefinition.upgradedCreatureId and FamilySpec.upgraded are required fields. Add safe optional-upgrade support across content construction, recruitment, encyclopedia, army training and serialization. Do not manufacture six identical “upgraded” clones to satisfy the current types. Existing two-form families must retain their current behavior.

Hiring sites have finite per-week stock shared between players for that site. Re-entering, capturing, loading or changing ownership cannot refill it. Validate stock, resources, destination capacity and ownership atomically. Recruit all affordable calculates legal quantities and exact aggregate cost before confirmation; it must not discard existing stacks or recruit into an invalid destination. Show a useful full-army or insufficient-resource state. A town without an appropriate neutral site does not gain these units automatically.

Use one or two meaningful hiring opportunities on most middle maps, at most three on larger regions. Follow each mission card's neutral field; many intentionally reuse existing centaurs/slingers/etc. Cross-cultural habitat placement is allowed: a Root-Runner can appear in a Celtic forest. New species should not appear everywhere because every campaign has a neutral icon.

7. ATLAS INTEGRATION — IMPORTANT

Current code in packages/presentation/src/sprites.ts describes horizontal uniform strips. This delivery instead supplies measured rectangles inside native multi-row atlases. A naive 4 × 6 grid will cut off several poses. Add a backward-compatible frame-region variant to the renderer, preserving the old strip format and all current sprites.

Each concrete animation has file, fps, loop and frames. Each frame has rect [sourceX, sourceY, width, height], anchor [pixelX,pixelY], sourceScale and sourceCell. At a fixed world ground position, draw the source rectangle at constant scale, offset by its anchor. Do not normalize each frame to its own bounding-box width/height: that makes the creature grow and shrink. Mirror the drawing transform around the same world anchor for left-facing units. Convert delivery file prefixes new-assets/ to runtime campaign-v2/ during registration.

Core states are idle, move, attack, defend, hit and death, four authored poses each. Idle/move loop. One-shot combat states play once; death holds its final pose. Victory explicitly aliases idle. Ability aliases defend for Pegasus/Baku/Afanc and attack for the others. No separate shoot or cast animation is needed for these non-shooters; only bind it if an actual rule calls it. The preview's repeated one-shot playback is a QA convenience, not game behavior.

Pegasus adds takeoff, fly and landing. The top-row third flight pose (zero-based sourceCell [0,2]) has a clipped wing and is excluded. Playback uses 3 takeoff + 4 fly + 4 landing poses. Do not register all 12 by grid count. The supplied .62 flight sourceScale is a starting visual match to its smaller core atlas; calibrate actual mount/body scale in game. Takeoff → looping flight → landing must return to the core ground anchor without a size jump. Flight animations do not change legal movement or permit illegal landings.

The new images are limited-frame painted sprite animations, not rigged 3D models or motion-capture sequences. They need timing/scale review against current units at the real zoom levels. Use high-resolution portraits for encyclopedia and recruitment detail, rather than magnifying a tiny battle frame into a full-screen illustration. Battle and map-guard rendering can use measured atlas frames; no new eight-direction art is supplied or required for the fixed-view presentation.

Never let visual animation completion determine damage, initiative, death, ability charges or movement legality. Those are deterministic rules events. Save/replay must work with animation disabled and reduced-motion enabled. Preserve transparent edges; do not replace alpha with black. Inspect on dark and light terrain. Source images have not been code-retouched or upscaled.

9. PERSISTENT MULTI-DAY HERO JOURNEYS

Keep each hero's selected destination and planned route at day end. Next day, the same hero can Continue journey without selecting the destination again. Save the destination, target/site identity where applicable, and route intent per hero, not in one global selected-hero variable. Save/load and switching heroes preserve it.

Recompute the remaining legal route when movement resumes using current terrain, gate/sluice state and knowledge. Do not grant extra movement or walk through a newly blocked cell. If the destination moves, vanishes or becomes unreachable, pause and explain why; do not silently redirect the hero to another objective or clear other heroes' routes. A player-selected new destination explicitly replaces that hero's plan. Retreat, defeat, teleport and mission transitions require deliberate route invalidation/recalculation. The next-day control continues only with the player's normal movement command; do not force movement on End turn.
