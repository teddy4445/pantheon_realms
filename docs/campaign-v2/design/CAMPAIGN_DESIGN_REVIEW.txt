PANTHEON REALMS — CAMPAIGN STORY AND MAP DESIGN REVIEW

2026-10-14



Discussion draft. Original design proposals informed by the current project and published Heroes campaign material. No game code, visuals or implementation handoff have been produced.



37 proposed playable missions, preserving story beats from all 45 current missions. This is a consolidation option, not an approved deletion or save migration. The existing project has a 3-mission tutorial plus six 7-mission campaigns. Proposed distribution: tutorial 2; Greek 6; Norse 6; Egyptian 6; Japanese 6; Celtic 6; Mesoamerican 5. If 45 scenarios are preferred, the seven merged cards can be split at their documented source-chapter boundaries; those split versions would still need separate pacing review.



RECOMMENDATION

The six realms originally belonged to separate worlds. A mysterious collision joined them. Their gods attempted an agreement and failed; direct divine war would unravel the already damaged world. Existing human champions now pursue their gods' claims through alliances, conquest and mythical armies. Keep the cause of the collision unresolved for now. Each regional campaign solves a concrete local crisis and reveals one aspect of the collision, without making a new seventh villain responsible for every problem.



CURRENT PROJECT AUDIT

The source contains 45 missions across seven campaigns. Mission cards retain a source chapter mapping; review numbers 01–37 are not new runtime IDs.

Five faction campaigns are built through arc.ts, sharing the Restore / Hunt / Covenant / Seek / Rival / Refuge / Finale sequence derived from the Winter Oath structure. The repeated story function is as important a problem as the terrain.

The shared layouts repeatedly use vertical river or mountain strips, one or two openings, and successive gates. Merely rotating them would preserve the same experience.

Existing encounters expose Quick battle and an exact-result preview. The new campaign rule must apply to the actual resolution path as well as the visible button; this review does not claim that it has already been changed.

This was a source and documentation review, not a completed playthrough of every current mission or a new playtest of Heroes III–V. Timing, battle counts and balance below are design targets.



HEROES III: STRATEGIC GEOGRAPHY AND BOUNDED CONTINUITY

Liberation allows Deal with the Devil and Neutral Affairs to be tackled in either order before the final scenario. That is a useful model for meaningful chapter order without an uncontrolled branching script. Hero continuity can reward investment, while scenario limits keep later maps from becoming trivial. Borrow route trade-offs, alternative chapter order and visible consequences; avoid fragile required-artifact carryover and mandatory cleanup of every corner.

HEROES IV: THE CAMPAIGN PROTAGONIST MUST CHANGE

The Price of Peace develops Emilia from a displaced civilian into a ruler and tests peace against coercion through Gavin Magnus and Solmyr. The useful lesson is that a campaign's central conflict should become more difficult as the hero gains power. Give each existing Pantheon Realms protagonist a distinct responsibility, recurring relationships and a final decision that follows from earlier practical choices.

HEROES V: OBJECTIVES CAN EVOLVE AFTER THE OPENING

The official manual describes objectives added during missions. The Ranger campaign's The Defense combines a home-front threat with the need to act beyond the capital. Borrow announced shifts, purposeful enemy pressure and chapter-specific set pieces. Avoid copying relentless waves or surprise failure conditions: the player should understand an emerging crisis before it becomes irreversible.

WHAT THIS REVIEW DOES NOT ASSUME

The Heroes campaigns are not uniformly nonlinear, and not every old quest gate or timer is desirable. An authored chapter sequence can remain coherent while each individual region offers several routes and strategies. The recommendations below are original proposals, not reproductions of Heroes plots or claims that every cited game used every proposed system.



CAMPAIGN EXPERIENCE RULES



CAMPAIGN COMBAT: MANUAL ONLY

Every battle involving the human campaign side—field, neutral, siege, garrison or scripted—opens tactical combat. Remove Quick battle, instant resolve, exact automatic casualty previews and any full-battle autoplay/auto-finish bypass in this mode. Keep animation-speed options, ordinary enemy AI turns, retreat and surrender under normal rules. AI-versus-AI world simulation need not become a battle the player must watch. Pre-battle diplomacy, paid passage, surrender or neutral joining are explicit encounter outcomes, not a hidden combat simulation. Mandatory story fights cannot disappear through a routine flee/join roll. Skirmish rules are outside this requested change.



EVERY MAP IS A PLACE WITH SEVERAL CONNECTIONS

Build the road/region graph before placing decorations. Except for the opening tutorial minutes, provide at least one complete circuit, two approaches to the principal objective, and a useful shortcut that opens through play. Give each route a visible difference in distance, safety, income or ally access. Dead ends are short optional reward pockets. Roads join settlement gates, farms, bridges and landings logically; water and mountains shape regions instead of acting as repeated vertical fences.



NONLINEARITY NEEDS CHOICES, NOT MORE WALKING

A medium map should offer roughly three to five meaningful regions and a large map five to seven. A useful event, information point or decision should normally appear within one to two movement days. Start near a junction or within a contested region as often as at an edge. Avoid routes that split cosmetically and immediately rejoin with identical cost. Completed places can become depots, refuges or threatened supply links, giving return visits a purpose.



ONE MAIN HERO CAN OPERATE THE WHOLE SCENARIO

Keep the existing protagonist and portrait cast. Dialogue companions are not silently promoted to new playable heroes. Outpost ownership, garrisoning, one-time deliveries and secured routes can advance objectives without requiring simultaneous occupation by three heroes. Any optional additional commander must already exist in the roster and must not be essential to a quest.



DISTINCT MISSION FORMATS

Mix ordinary town-building maps with limited-force expeditions, preparation followed by defence, resource logistics, investigation, political contests, a planned withdrawal and counterattack, and rescue through changing terrain. Do not require a new town, a fixed recruitment quota, two shrine switches and a final gate in every chapter. Six regional finales may contain major battles, but their preparation and stakes should differ.



FINITE, UNDERSTANDABLE OPPOSITION

Opponents defend income, move supplies, reclaim useful positions or pursue an announced objective. Give forces visible origins and bounded reserves. Announce important raids two or three movement days ahead where feasible. Do not create unlimited offscreen armies or have enemies know unexplored hero positions. A failed optional defence changes a capped bonus or later encounter; it does not silently delete the campaign's only quest item.



COMBAT DENSITY MATCHES MANUAL PLAY

Target about 3–4 required battles in early maps, 4–6 in most middle maps and 5–8 in finales, with only a few optional encounters. These are targets to playtest, not a promise of final difficulty. Low-value mines and resource piles do not all need guards. Every required fight should teach or test something distinct: approach under fire, protect a lane, break a reserve, hold a landing, take a fortified position or defeat a mixed formation.



CAMPAIGN CHOICES HAVE BOUNDED CONSEQUENCES

Carry selected story flags and limited support forward: a garrison, one purchase, information, a reserve reduction or an ending scene. Do not accumulate the entire army and economy across all maps. Preserve existing hero progression deliberately, with disclosed chapter caps and starting-force rules. Neither optional chapter order nor a discarded quest item can make a later map unwinnable. Each optional-order pair requires briefings for both sequences and a bounded reward, not two contradictory histories.



CLEAR FAILURE AND RECOVERY

Protagonist defeat is a normal campaign loss. Losing a required defended objective during a declared defence may also be a loss, but the rule must be visible before commitment. Ordinary town loss should allow recovery unless the scenario explicitly says otherwise. A complete elimination state follows the game's disclosed rule. Record evidence, completed rescues and fulfilled agreements permanently. Provide a pre-set-piece save opportunity; never require a hidden calendar deadline.



TRAVEL ACROSS DAYS

Retain each hero's chosen destination and remaining route when movement runs out, through End Day and save/load. On the next day the player can Continue Route rather than mark the same destination again. Revalidate only the affected route when terrain, ownership or obstacles change; pause before a new hostile encounter and explain any reroute. Never auto-enter a campaign battle or spend a new day's movement without the player's chosen continuation behaviour.



GODS, EXISTING HEROES AND VISUAL SCOPE

Use the six existing protagonists and named supporting cast shown under each campaign. Gods communicate through existing portraits, dialogue, shrines and consequences; they do not appear as new directly fighting god units. New neutral species are proposals only. No new hero portraits, cinematic bosses, UI concepts or terrain art are requested at this stage. New settlement or site functions should first reuse existing buildings and landmarks.



NEW MECHANICS ARE PROPOSALS, NOT PRESENT CAPABILITIES

Player-controlled sluices, scheduled route changes, automatic evacuation progress, district support, mission-order dialogue, conditional surrender and supply-driven enemy behaviour may need new campaign scripting. The current objective system already supports combinations of site progress, flags, counters and other conditions, but that does not make every scenario below immediately supported. Preserve each design's intent; after approval, separately scope engineering and any truly missing visuals.



NO ARTIFICIAL WAITING

A timed phase must have a visible clock and meaningful actions during the wait. Prefer a player-started event after preparation. Stable fallback routes prevent stranding when ice, water or seasons change. Do not require repeatedly clicking End Day merely to catch a ferry. Where naval traversal is not available in the current mission systems, use existing landings as explicit ferry links; this proposal does not require a new ship combat system.



REVIEW AND BALANCE GATES

Before approval for production, test every map with the principal route, the longer alternate route, both chapter orders where allowed, minimum optional rewards and an interrupted multi-day journey. Verify one-hero feasibility, finite enemy reserves, recruit stock limits, no required-new-creature dependency, no accidental auto-resolve access, and readable objective/failure updates. Mission sizes and 35–115-minute estimates assume Normal difficulty and must be revised after playtests.



SIX NEUTRAL CREATURE PROPOSALS

Here 'neutral' means unaffiliated, not restricted to ordinary real animals. All six can be hired by any pantheon where habitat and scenario placement make sense. Use one or two meaningful hiring sites on most middle maps, with a few larger maps offering three; do not flood every map with all six. Weekly stocks are finite and shared for that site; ownership changes must not refill them. Unlock by a one-time agreement, resource delivery or manual encounter. Prices and stock should make them sidegrades, not a cheap replacement for faction tier progression. Existing army-slot, morale and movement rules still apply. Every required objective remains achievable using existing units. Several mission cards use existing neutrals specifically to avoid making new creature production a prerequisite for the redesign.



PEGASUS — Greek

The winged horse of Greek mythology; turning the named mythical creature into a recruitable species is a game adaptation.

Combat: Fast flying support. A once-per-battle protective intervention for a nearby ally makes it different from a damage-focused griffin. Modest damage and vulnerability to ranged attacks.

Habitat: High meadows, coastal cliffs and open uplands. Hire at a winged-horse meadow, initially after a local protection task.

Initially one unit, no mandatory upgraded form.

https://www.theoi.com/Ther/HipposPegasos.html



ROOT-RUNNER — Norse-inspired

A proposed species inspired by Ratatoskr, the squirrel messenger of the world tree. Root-Runner is an original game name, not a claimed traditional creature species.

Combat: Fast fragile scout/skirmisher with a single decoy or distraction. No permanent extra adventure movement, free scouting of the entire map or bypassing quest barriers.

Habitat: Old forests, mountain tree lines and great-root groves. May migrate into Celtic woodland after the collision.

Initially one unit. Keep its combat role useful without adding a new movement subsystem.

https://www.germanicmythology.com/PoeticEdda/GRM32.html



SERPOPARD — Ancient Egyptian art

Long-necked feline imagery appears on early Egyptian ceremonial palettes. Serpopard is a modern descriptive name; a specific ancient Egyptian name or meaning should not be asserted.

Combat: Ground reach/control creature. A short two-hex strike creates a distinctive line threat, balanced by weak flanks and moderate durability.

Habitat: Desert-river boundaries, dry ravines and quarry edges. Hire at a shaded den beside reliable water.

Initially one unit. Reach and retaliation rules require later balance review.

https://www.ashmolean.org/two-dog-palette



BAKU — Japanese

The nightmare-devouring creature represented in Japanese tradition and art.

Combat: Slow support with one limited fear/nightmare cleanse or protective action per battle. Avoid unlimited healing or a universal magic dispel.

Habitat: Mist forests, old shrines and quiet woodland clearings. Hire through a dream-sanctuary encounter.

Initially one unit; cleansing only affects explicitly compatible statuses.

https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/59035



AFANC — Welsh / Celtic

A lake monster in Welsh folklore, with variable descriptions. Select a coherent animal-like design later rather than claiming one definitive historical appearance.

Combat: Slow defensive water-beast with a local guard/control ability. Strong at holding a lane, poor at pursuit; no creation of walkable water.

Habitat: Freshwater lakes, reed basins and deep river pools. A protected pool supplies a small hire stock.

Initially one unit. Amphibious flavour need not grant unrestricted adventure-map water movement.

https://coflein.gov.uk/en/sites/402570



AHUITZOTL — Nahua / Mesoamerican

A dangerous water creature described in the Florentine Codex tradition, associated with springs and waterways. Use a tail-hand silhouette, distinct from the existing cenote crocodile.

Combat: Mobile ambush/control unit with a limited one-hex pull against an eligible target. Validate destination and size; no pulling into instant-death terrain or through walls.

Habitat: Cenotes, springs, jungle lakes and reed channels. Hire at a warded spring after a local water agreement.

Initially one unit. Do not treat Nahua tradition as interchangeable with every Mesoamerican culture.

https://florentinecodex.getty.edu/en/book/11/folio/70v?nhTexts=&spTexts=



THE 37 MISSIONS — ALL STORY CHANGES ARE PROPOSALS

THE THREE BEACONS

Hero: Alexios

Existing cast: Thaleia, Ione, Melas, Scout Captain

A practical coastal rescue becomes a lesson in who controls the new world's roads.

Alexios begins by treating light as a military asset. Thaleia and Ione show him that towns need a network they can maintain themselves. Melas argues that one commander must control it. The tutorial ends with that conflict unresolved rather than with a generic victory speech.

Order: Local chapters 1 → 2. Global missions 01–02. Two tutorial scenarios preserve the three existing beacon chapters.



01. The Silent Shore — Wings over the Pass

Small coastal bowl; 35–50 minutes; 3–4 required battles.

Current source: threeBeacons.ts; chapters 1 + 2; The Silent Shore + Wings over the Pass

Story and revelation: Alexios finds that the lighthouse and the mountain beacon have failed for different reasons: raiders cut the coastal supply road, while frightened griffins have abandoned the pass. Thaleia needs safe movement for ordinary people before anyone can march an army.

Primary tasks: Train the town watch, restore the Outer Lighthouse, and reopen the griffin nesting ground; finish by lighting the pass beacon. Teach recruitment and one upgrade without imposing a large repeated recruitment quota.

Map layout and routes: Asterion sits inside a horseshoe bay, with the lighthouse on its western horn and the aerie inland above the eastern horn. A short, guarded tidal causeway and a longer orchard road connect both sides. Opening a hillside stair completes a triangle back to town. The first objective is visible from the start; the inland objective is revealed after the lighthouse.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: One clearly announced raider patrol threatens the supply road. It has a home camp and cannot respawn indefinitely. The aerie encounter teaches using cover against ranged enemies.

Optional choice and consequence: Repair the causeway first for a faster army route, or provision the hillside hamlet first for a small garrison bonus in the next mission. Both objectives remain achievable.

Neutral hiring: An existing centaur camp in the upland meadow offers a small optional hire after a local favour.

Victory and failure: Alexios must survive. Victory requires both beacons and the opened nesting ground; no need to clear every guard. Losing the starting town is recoverable while Alexios has an army.



02. The Storm Beacon

Small/medium headland ring; 45–60 minutes; 3–5 battles.

Current source: threeBeacons.ts; chapters 3; The Storm Beacon

Story and revelation: Melas has taken the great lens because he believes the lighthouse network must answer to a single commander. Ione exposes the cost: isolated settlements can no longer signal for help. Alexios defeats his blockade, but Melas survives to matter in the Greek campaign.

Primary tasks: Recover the lens from Melas, carry it to the Storm Beacon, and defend the repair crew for one tactical battle. Only one relay is needed to power the beacon; the second is an optional support objective.

Map layout and routes: Highwatch stands on the inner edge of a crescent headland. Melas occupies a central plateau reached by a defended ridge road or an outer coastal switchback. The north and south relays connect across the plateau, making a complete circuit. The beacon lies above Highwatch, so the final journey returns through a changed home region rather than reaching the far-right edge.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: The Scout Captain moves from Melas's camp to a signalled relay target. Taking the camp removes that reinforcement source. The final battle protects the beacon approach, not a fragile new escort unit.

Optional choice and consequence: Restore both relays to strengthen the repair defence, or take the direct assault before Melas completes his field fortification. Victory and Melas's survival are fixed; the network's local or military management changes the epilogue.

Neutral hiring: Existing hill slingers occupy a disused coastal quarry.

Victory and failure: Alexios survives; lens installed; repair defence won. A failed optional relay does not block the campaign.

THE SILENT ORACLE

Hero: Alexios

Existing cast: Thaleia, Ione, Myrrine, Melas, Theron, Doros, Phaidon, Scout Captain

A political investigation into who is entitled to speak for gods who cannot safely act directly.

Theron sealed a real, unstable oracle spring and exploited its silence to prevent rival cities from fighting—then used forged prophecies to demand obedience. Alexios must expose the fraud without pretending the divine danger is imaginary. Melas is a rival with a possible negotiated surrender, not a repeated disposable boss.

Order: Local 1 → {2 and 3 in either order} → {4 and 5 in either order} → 6. Both chapters in each pair are required. Global missions 03–08.



03. The Pilgrims’ Road

Medium three-town basin; 45–65 minutes; 3–4 battles.

Current source: silentOracle.ts; chapters 1; The Pilgrims’ Road

Story and revelation: Elaia blames its neighbours for the Oracle's silence. Alexios finds contradictory prophecies stamped with the same seal. Thaleia asks for witnesses; Ione asks for a road people can actually use.

Primary tasks: Reopen travel between Elaia and two pilgrim shelters, obtain two independent testimonies from three possible sources, and expose the Hushed road captain. Evidence is recorded permanently when found.

Map layout and routes: Elaia starts near the centre-south of a branching river basin. One shelter lies in a northern olive valley, another on a western hill, and the captain's customs post occupies the eastern confluence. Three crossings form a loop; repairing one adds a shortcut but does not unlock the whole map. A neutral market links the outer roads.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: The captain extorts a different road every few days, visibly reducing that road's supply reward rather than deleting witnesses. Defeating his finite patrols weakens his final encounter.

Optional choice and consequence: Spend supplies sheltering pilgrims for later local support, or buy the captain's ledger to expose his financiers sooner. Either route can establish the same core truth.

Neutral hiring: Existing centaurs hire from the dry meadow beyond the olive groves.

Victory and failure: Alexios survives; two testimonies and two safe shelter connections; captain defeated. The revelation opens the countryside and archive investigations in either order.



04. The Bronze Bull and the Eyrie

Medium basin around a mountain; 60–80 minutes; 4–5 battles.

Current source: silentOracle.ts; chapters 2 + 3; The Bronze Bull + The Eyrie of Aetos

Story and revelation: The bronze bull is devastating Thymbra's fields while the griffin keeper profits from closed mountain travel. Myrrine's warning suggests that the two crises share a patron, but their victims need different solutions.

Primary tasks: Stop the bronze bull, secure grain access to Thymbra, and obtain the aerie's cooperation. The bull is a distinctive battle using existing constructs; the keeper can be defeated or paid through a substantial local contract.

Map layout and routes: A mountain divides a northern nesting shelf from a southern agricultural plain. Thymbra sits west of it; a shrine and horse pasture sit east. The orchard road, quarry pass and outer meadow form two connected loops. The bull moves between two feeding grounds; its location is scouted, not fixed as a gate.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: Each announced raid damages one harvest bonus, with a capped loss. The keeper's patrols guard trade, not every route. The player can confront either problem first.

Optional choice and consequence: Restoring the fields grants provisions for a later siege; repairing the mountain trade route grants griffin support instead. Both regions are rescued, but scarce reconstruction funds determine the lasting benefit.

Neutral hiring: A Pegasus meadow offers an optional pact after the bull is stopped. The campaign remains balanced without this proposed new creature.

Victory and failure: Bull defeated and aerie cooperation established. Neither a specific purchased army composition nor a compulsory upgrade is required.



05. The Hall of Echoes

Medium expedition map; 60–75 minutes; 3–5 battles. No starting town; one friendly resupply landing.

Current source: silentOracle.ts; chapters 4; The Hall of Echoes

Story and revelation: The archive contains the Oracle's genuine warnings and the edits used to turn them into commands. Theron ordered the spring sealed after a real fracture; his later certainty was manufactured.

Primary tasks: Enter with a capped expedition force, secure two of three surviving records, and extract to Halia. Two one-use resupply caches are guaranteed; the Scout Captain is optional if another extraction route is secured.

Map layout and routes: Halia occupies the lagoon's southern shore; the archive sits on the central island. A northern ferry chain, western raised causeway and eastern marsh landing provide different access. The sea-chain fort controls the fastest crossing, not the only one. Opening an archive service landing creates a short return loop.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: The captain prepares a publicly timed archive seizure. Missing the early window relocates one record to his fort rather than destroying the campaign's evidence. No unannounced quest-item loss.

Optional choice and consequence: Preserve the archive intact through a longer extraction route for Myrrine's later research, or expose the records immediately at Halia for stronger public backing. Both retain the essential revelation.

Neutral hiring: Existing clay golems can be hired at a restored workshop on the inland shore.

Victory and failure: Two records delivered and Alexios alive. If this mission is played before the countryside chapter, its evidence changes the keeper's dialogue and grants one negotiation discount; no combat snowball.



06. The Return of Melas

Medium contested valley with a central city; 65–85 minutes; 4–6 battles.

Current source: silentOracle.ts; chapters 5; The Return of Melas

Story and revelation: Melas now protects Pyrgos from raiders and uses that real achievement to justify permanent military rule. Showing him the forged prophecies shakes his loyalty, but does not automatically settle the struggle for the city.

Primary tasks: Break Melas's control over two of three supply districts; then take Pyrgos or compel his negotiated surrender through evidence and protected civilian supply. Any final fought siege is manual.

Map layout and routes: Pyrgos occupies the middle of a broad valley. Alexios enters from a southern foothill camp. Three district roads circle the city and reconnect beyond it; a hill road bypasses the main gate at a significant travel cost. Farms, quarry and bridge tolls each support a different part of Melas's army.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: Melas chooses between protecting income and threatening the player's base. His forces come from visible holdings, with a capped emergency reserve. Raiding a district changes his deployment.

Optional choice and consequence: Accept a negotiated surrender and retain a limited local garrison, or defeat him and place the city under civilian council control for a later legitimacy bonus. Neither grants an extra playable hero.

Neutral hiring: Pegasi inhabit a remote ridge; existing brigand deserters can be hired after their camp is disarmed.

Victory and failure: Pyrgos changes allegiance and civilians retain one working supply road. Melas survives as an existing character; the outcome changes his final testimony.



07. The Labyrinth of Kerata

Medium crater and quarry network; 55–75 minutes; 4–5 battles.

Current source: silentOracle.ts; chapters 6; The Labyrinth of Kerata

Story and revelation: Doros has imprisoned minotaurs as labour and as convenient proof that Theron's enemies are monsters. Their release can undermine the regime more effectively than another destroyed town.

Primary tasks: Release three captive groups in any order, secure one refuge, and defeat Doros or destroy his capacity to retake the prison by capturing its guard posts. A return visit to a liberated wing reveals witness testimony.

Map layout and routes: A sunken quarry has an outer rim road, two ramps and a lower ring of galleries. The prison yards form three spokes with cross-passages; none is the final room of a long corridor. Opening winches creates new connections rather than another serial key gate. The refuge sits near the original entrance.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: Doros dispatches finite recapture parties towards visible occupied yards. Released groups travel as abstracted progress along secured routes; the player need not babysit three fragile escort units simultaneously.

Optional choice and consequence: Escort the first group to safety before continuing for stronger public testimony, or arm it to secure the remaining yards for a small military reward. All prisoners can still be saved.

Neutral hiring: Existing ogre labourers hire from an abandoned work camp; no mandatory new creature.

Victory and failure: All three groups freed and a refuge secured; Alexios survives. The choice supplies a bounded finale benefit, not a required key.



08. The Spring of Kalliste

Large sanctuary basin; 80–110 minutes; 5–7 battles.

Current source: silentOracle.ts; chapters 7; The Spring of Kalliste

Story and revelation: Theron offers a bargain: accept his single voice and he will contain the dangerous spring. Alexios must prove that the cities can protect it without surrendering their judgement. Myrrine confirms that uncontrolled divine intervention would tear the merged world.

Primary tasks: Secure a spring approach, protect Myrrine's stabilisation at two sites in either order, and defeat Theron's final host. Phaidon guards a reserve depot that can be bypassed or neutralised.

Map layout and routes: The spring lies at the centre of a circular mountain basin. A pilgrim town, prison road and cliff settlement occupy three surrounding sectors. Inner and outer rings connect them through three passes. The final sanctuary can be approached from several sectors; securing more sectors changes enemy reserves instead of unlocking a mandatory gate.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: Theron reallocates troops between the sanctuary and exposed supply sites. Each ritual triggers one announced counterattack. Stabilisation persists once completed; the player is not forced to hold multiple points with one hero.

Optional choice and consequence: After victory, preserve the Oracle as an independent adviser or place the spring under a council of cities. Prior witnesses, prisoners and Melas shape the ceremony and who trusts the settlement.

Neutral hiring: One optional mixed neutral refuge offers a small choice of existing units or Pegasi, with a shared finite stock.

Victory and failure: Theron defeated, both stabilisations complete, Alexios and the spring preserved. Neither ending is a simple divine-power reward.

THE WINTER OATH

Hero: Hervor

Existing cast: Eydis, Skeggi, Ulfhild, Hrafn the Hollow, Bone-Helm, Barrow Captain, The Grey Jarl

An inherited bargain, an overdue winter, and a leader responsible for the weapon that disturbed it.

Hervor's sword awakened an oath that once protected vulnerable settlements but now imprisons them. Ulfhild profits from closed harbours yet has people to feed. The Grey Jarl demands the kingdom he was promised. The question is how to honour protection without accepting perpetual ownership.

Order: Local 1 → 2 → {3 and 4 in either order} → 5 → 6. Ice on the Sound and The Jarl of Hvitstrand have order-aware briefings. Global missions 09–14.



09. Barrow Light

Small/medium valley hub; 45–60 minutes; 3–4 battles.

Current source: winterOath.ts; chapters 1; Barrow Light

Story and revelation: The barrow opened when Hervor drew her sword. Eydis finds an oath promising protection through winter, with no agreed end. Skeggi needs to evacuate exposed farms before another night raid.

Primary tasks: Relight the barrow watch, secure two evacuation routes, and recover the oath's first inscription. The watch is a defensive position, not merely a switch.

Map layout and routes: Kaldvik sits at the junction of three valleys. The barrow overlooks the north; farms occupy west and east branches linked by a forest trail. A repaired ford shortens the circuit through town. An optional ridge reveals the next raid's origin.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: Two finite night raids are announced before they depart. Guarding a route with a town-controlled outpost counts; Hervor need not stand in two places. Failure costs a capped optional provision bonus.

Optional choice and consequence: Shelter families inside Kaldvik for a later defensive reserve, or help them reach the coast for supplies in the harbour chapter.

Neutral hiring: Existing hill slingers hire at a logging camp.

Victory and failure: Hervor survives, inscription recovered, both evacuation routes secured. A missed bonus does not fail the campaign.



10. The White Pack and the Valkyrie’s Price

Medium interconnected uplands; 60–80 minutes; 4–5 battles.

Current source: winterOath.ts; chapters 2 + 3; The White Pack + The Valkyrie’s Price

Story and revelation: The wolves are pursuing the same restless dead that imprison the valkyries. Ulfhild has bought silence with grain while lower villages starve. Hervor must break the cycle without treating every wild creature as an enemy.

Primary tasks: End the pack's attacks, free the valkyrie refuge, and settle access through the troll pass by payment or battle. The pack's lead battle can be avoided through a costly food-and-territory agreement; the barrow jailer cannot.

Map layout and routes: Birkaby lies below a forked mountain. Western forest, eastern scree and a high saddle form a figure eight. The troll pass is the fastest connector; a longer traversable lower route always exists. Pack tracks point towards two hunting grounds.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: A finite pack changes grounds after a signalled hunt. The refuge jailer prepares one reinforcement, delayed by interrupting his grave-road supply.

Optional choice and consequence: Give scarce grain to the villages for stronger harbour support, or supply the refuge for a one-time valkyrie contingent. Neither denies the other region its essential rescue.

Neutral hiring: A Root-Runner grove on the lower forest loop introduces the proposed neutral scout.

Victory and failure: Attacks ended, refuge freed, pass access resolved. Progress does not require owning a particular premium creature.



11. Ice on the Sound

Medium naval expedition; 65–85 minutes; 4–6 battles. One friendly resupply island, no full town-building phase.

Current source: winterOath.ts; chapters 4; Ice on the Sound

Story and revelation: Bone-Helm's ship carries the names of people bound to the old oath. Eydis discovers that destroying the ship without recovering its ledger would leave those families bound forever. If Hvitstrand has already been opened, its merchants supply an extra landing; otherwise the expedition establishes that route itself.

Primary tasks: Recover the ledger, establish two safe landing points, and defeat Bone-Helm. The Norns' Well supplies interpretation, not a third compulsory key.

Map layout and routes: Three principal islands surround a navigable sound, with short ferries and a longer sheltered southern route. A central ice crossing opens on a visible schedule but is never the only exit. Each landing connects to a second route. No army is trapped by ice changing beneath it.

Itzel discovers that the new fire failed because cities were drawing strength from one another through the merged world's fractures. Tecuani offers central control; Itztli intends to monopolize the surviving flame. Xoco's knowledge makes a shared renewal possible, but a central hearth offers faster security at a political cost.

Order: Local 1 → 2 → {3 and 4 in either order} → 5. The political contest and mountain rescue can swap; neither requires the other's reward. Global missions 33–37.



33. The Cold Altar and the Black Jaguars

Medium jungle-lake frontier; 60–80 minutes; 4–5 battles.

Current source: newFire.ts; chapters 1 + 2; The Cold Altar + The Black Jaguars

Story and revelation: Xochitla's fire has died before the appointed night. Jaguars displaced by disturbances at the old altar attack the cacao groves. Grandmother Xoco recognises a fault in the merged world's shared sources of power.

Primary tasks: Restore a safe route between two settlements, end the jaguar attacks and recover the altar's ember record. This introduces the crisis through ordinary supply and travel before cosmic explanation.

Map layout and routes: Xochitla and Tepeyolo face each other across a crescent lake. A short causeway, long jungle ridge and canoe crossing make a triangle. Groves lie on both shores; the altar sits on the inner peninsula with two approaches.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: Jaguars move between scouted hunting grounds. One finite raider band attempts to seize an ember shipment; losing its optional cargo changes a reward, not campaign access.

Optional choice and consequence: Return the first provisions to growers for later local support, or fund shared watch posts for a defensive bonus.

Neutral hiring: An Ahuitzotl spring offers a dangerous optional recruitment pact after its water is protected; existing wild boars provide a cheaper option elsewhere.

Victory and failure: Route secured, attacks ended and record recovered; Itzel survives. The record points to the eagle keepers and the lake's missing custodians.



34. The House of Eagles and the House of Bats

Medium/large highland-lake network; 75–95 minutes; 4–6 battles.

Current source: newFire.ts; chapters 3 + 4; The House of Eagles + The House of Bats

Story and revelation: The eagle keepers retain the calendar, while Acatl's lake network holds the testimony of the disappeared. Itzel needs both to distinguish a failed ritual from deliberate theft. Chimal argues for speed; Xoco insists on understanding the cost.

Primary tasks: Earn access to the eagle calendar, free the cenote sanctuary and retrieve two witness records from Acatl's lake holdings. The sanctuary reuses existing cenote crocodile content.

Map layout and routes: Ocelotepec stands on a western ridge; the House of Bats occupies a central island. Northern stairs, southern shore road and two canoe routes create overlapping circuits. The eagle terrace can be visited before or after the lake; neither is a key for the other.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: Acatl relocates between visible docks with a finite escort. Taking one depot exposes a faster crossing; taking both removes his reserve but costs time.

Optional choice and consequence: Publish the recovered calendar for wider cooperation, or use it first to secure a military agreement with the keepers. Each offers one limited bonus against Tecuani.

Neutral hiring: Ahuitzotl hire at a restored spring; existing wyverns occupy an optional ridge.

Victory and failure: Calendar obtained, sanctuary freed and two records recovered. Acatl's personal defeat is optional if the records and routes are secured.



35. The Lord of Itzcalli

Medium city-and-canal network; 70–90 minutes; 4–6 battles.

Current source: newFire.ts; chapters 5; The Lord of Itzcalli

Story and revelation: Tecuani offers to save the next age by owning its only fire. His city is genuinely organised and well supplied. Itzel must show the surrounding settlements that cooperation can survive without his monopoly.

Primary tasks: Deliver a declared reconstruction contract to two independent settlements while preventing Tecuani from controlling both principal causeways. Then confront his field army or force a surrender through completed contracts and exposed hoarding. The city siege is an available alternative, not the default finish.

Map layout and routes: Itzcalli occupies a central lake platform. Cacao, stone and reed districts form a triangular shore network joined by causeways and boats. A distant ridge offers a slower protected approach. The city has two feasible assault landings.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: Tecuani runs a visible competing supply operation and can raid a route with a finite army. His deliveries strengthen his final force up to a displayed cap; they do not trigger sudden defeat. Capturing one depot delays his next delivery.

Optional choice and consequence: Retain a central store under public oversight for immediate stability, or distribute reserves among the districts for resilience. Neither option removes essential final-mission supplies.

Neutral hiring: Ahuitzotl inhabit the reed channels; existing ogres hire in an abandoned quarry.

Victory and failure: Two contracts delivered and Tecuani's monopoly ended by surrender or a manual battle. Itzel survives.



36. The Fire Mountain

Medium volcanic ring with three radial approaches; 65–85 minutes; 4–6 battles.

Current source: newFire.ts; chapters 6; The Fire Mountain

Story and revelation: Mazatl chains the xiuhcoatl to extract enough fire for one dominant city. The mountain is becoming unstable. Itzel must free the creatures and secure a usable ember without turning the rescue into a race through an arbitrary corridor.

Primary tasks: Prepare a safe containment site, break two of three extraction works and defeat Mazatl at the crater. Choose when to initiate the final release.

Map layout and routes: A stable outer road circles the volcano. Quarry road, forest saddle and ash ravine lead inward, with two cross-links on the mid-slope. Each extraction work feeds a different defensive reserve. Lava warnings close only optional shortcuts; the outer road stays safe.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: A visible eruption phase begins only after the player starts the release. It changes the final approach and schedules one counterattack; it never instantly kills the army on a map tile.

Optional choice and consequence: Save the mountain's workshops for a later material reserve, or dismantle all extraction for stronger creature cooperation. Both release the captives.

Neutral hiring: Existing clay golems hire at a cooled quarry; Ahuitzotl remain restricted to the lowland spring.

Victory and failure: Containment prepared, two works disabled, Mazatl defeated and ember secured. Existing xiuhcoatl are reused.



37. The Smoking Gate

Large linked city basins; 90–115 minutes; 5–8 battles.

Current source: newFire.ts; chapters 7; The Smoking Gate

Story and revelation: Itztli plans to make every surviving city dependent on a single stolen flame. Xoco's records reveal how several hearths can share the renewal safely. Itzel's final struggle is over the terms of the next age, not simply possession of a treasure.

Primary tasks: Prepare three of four hearths, cut Tlacatl's main supply route and defeat Itztli at the Smoking Gate. Launch the renewal only after selecting the final distribution plan.

Map layout and routes: Four city basins surround a central obsidian ridge. An outer caravan circuit and two inner passes link them. The Smoking Gate has a direct fortified approach and a longer approach through a prepared hearth region. Sites provide different final support rather than sequential permission keys.

Enemy behaviour and pressure: Itztli concentrates attacks on the richest prepared hearth. Finite defenders and allied garrisons allow one main hero to respond. The final ritual's schedule is visible before launch; exploration has no hidden hard deadline.

Optional choice and consequence: Distribute the new fire among protected local hearths, or establish a common central hearth with enforceable access. The finale shows how prior district promises affect trust. Both avoid reopening direct divine war.

Neutral hiring: A final mixed caravan offers one limited neutral purchase, including Ahuitzotl where appropriate.

Victory and failure: Three hearths prepared, supply route cut, Itztli defeated and renewal completed. Itzel survives. The concluding chronicle may reference outcomes from other completed campaigns; unplayed regions remain uncertain reports, so it neither spoils their decisions nor imposes a fixed order on the six campaigns.
