# Unit History, Mythology, and Original Game Lore

**Status:** P - editorial/content draft. Historical and ancient-literary notes are sourced; game-lore paragraphs below are original fiction for Pantheon Realms. This is not final localization, voice script, or a claim of cultural completeness.

## Editorial structure for every creature family

Store `sourceNote`, `sourceRefs`, `sourceStatus`, `gameLore`, and optional `upgradeLore` separately. The unit page must label which is which. An ancient text is evidence for a literary tradition, not evidence that its supernatural beings existed. Greek-inspired assets need not pretend all periods and local traditions formed one uniform army.

Upgrade names describe our progression system. They do not need a fabricated historical institution to justify them. No history paragraph may silently define an ability absent from the unit rules.

## Hoplite / Veteran Hoplite

**History note:** Greek hoplites are associated with armored infantry, a spear and shield, and fighting in close formation. Equipment and practice varied by place and period. Source: [L01](../reference/01_sources_and_version_scope.md), museum scholarship. This note does not establish our troop price, formation bonus, or recruitment speed.

**Game lore:** The road to Asterion is lined with shields bearing names rather than royal crests. Each belongs to a household that promised someone would stand when the warning bells rang. Hoplites learn to hold a narrow path together, trusting the shield beside them as much as their own spear. They are not the fastest travelers or the loudest heroes of the city's songs, but returning caravans know the sound of their watchfires being lit.

**Upgrade lore:** Veteran Hoplites teach the pause before a charge reaches the line. Their distinction is practiced steadiness, not a divine transformation.

## Peltast / Veteran Peltast

**History note:** Xenophon's *Hellenica* discusses Iphicrates and his peltasts in the Lechaeum campaign context. This supports using an ancient light-troop tradition as inspiration; the full equipment/period-specific visual brief still needs its own research. Source: [L06](../reference/01_sources_and_version_scope.md). Do not claim that every peltast fought identically or wore one standardized uniform.

**Game lore:** Peltasts learn the hills before they learn the parade ground. They carry only what lets them cross a dry riverbed, find a gap between shields, and return before a heavier enemy can close. Asterion's officers value them for the questions they force an opponent to answer: protect the flank, pursue the skirmishers, or keep advancing under fire. The best return with useful news and an unused last weapon.

**Upgrade lore:** Veteran Peltasts are trusted to choose their own firing ground and carry additional ammunition. Their improved performance is represented by the defined upgrade statistics, not an extra unlisted action.

## Centaur / Centaur Sentinel

**Mythology note:** Ancient accounts include named centaurs such as Pholus and Chiron as well as hostile centaur encounters. Apollodorus' account in *Library* 2.5.4 provides examples. Source: [L03](../reference/01_sources_and_version_scope.md). Our recruitable family and its political agreements are fictional adaptations, not a claim that all centaurs share one character.

**Game lore:** The grove's covenant is written on neither parchment nor stone. Its terms are paths left open, springs left clean, and warnings answered before smoke reaches the canopy. Centaurs who ride with the polis do not call its ruler their master. They follow an agreement between neighbors, carrying news from ridges that ordinary scouts reach only after sunset. Break the agreement in a story, and its consequences should be narrated as a choice rather than invented as a hidden statistic.

**Upgrade lore:** Sentinels know the old boundary routes and lead the first response when a covenant's roads are threatened.

## Griffin / Windcrest Griffin

**Mythology note:** Greek literary sources, including the Herodotus passages collected in L05, tell of griffins associated with guarded gold. Griffin imagery is not exclusively Greek. Source: [L05](../reference/01_sources_and_version_scope.md). The winged battlefield ally described below is our game interpretation.

**Game lore:** Above Griffin Pass, the nests face the first light. The first envoys brought gold and were driven from the cliffs; the next brought food and waited. The covenant began when a wounded fledgling was returned without chains. Now selected keepers climb the aerie's long stair, leaving offerings where a broad-winged shadow can inspect them. A griffin joins a battle on its own fierce terms, quick to answer an attack but careless of the neat rows preferred by ground commanders.

**Upgrade lore:** Windcrest Griffins are experienced guardians of the highest nests. Their trained battlefield persistence is expressed by the retaliation rule, not invulnerability.

## Cavalry / Companion Cavalry

**History note:** Xenophon's *Cavalry Commander* discusses horsemanship, terrain practice, supplies, and command. Source: [L07](../reference/01_sources_and_version_scope.md). This is inspiration for trained mounted troops, not evidence for our exact charge modifier. The generic upgrade name 'Companion Cavalry' is fictional here and should not be presented as a researched reconstruction of one historical corps.

**Game lore:** The court measures a rider by the condition of the horse at the journey's end. Young cavalry learn patience before speed: which track will hold after rain, where an enemy line can be approached, and when a charge would spend lives for nothing. Their arrival can decide a fight, but their daily work is less dramatic: escorting a grain cart, bringing a warning, and making sure the road remains a road rather than a border no one dares cross.

**Upgrade lore:** Companion riders train together until the commander's signal is almost unnecessary. Their new title represents trusted service within this fictional polis.

## Temple Adept / Temple Seer

**Source note:** This magical combat profession and its institutional structure are original Pantheon Realms fiction. Do not label it as a historical Greek military class or invent an ancient source for a defensive spell. Historical research may later inform clothing or architecture without changing this status.

**Game lore:** Temple Adepts keep the records that armies would rather forget: every promise made before a march and every name missing afterward. They study the ways a spoken vow steadies a frightened company, but the light they carry into battle belongs to this world's living gods. Some serve beside commanders, others among the troops, and the two roles must remain distinct. A hero called an Oracle is not the same entity as an Adept recruited from a town.

**Upgrade lore:** Seers learn to place a brief ward over their companions. The rite is shared by the whole company, explaining the family-wide charge rather than an unlimited ward from every individual.

## Minotaur / Labyrinth Warden

**Mythology note:** Apollodorus describes the Minotaur in the story of Pasiphae and the Labyrinth. Source: [L02](../reference/01_sources_and_version_scope.md). That named mythic being is not a historical account of a recruitable species. Our recurring Minotaur people are a deliberate fantasy expansion and must be labeled as such.

**Game lore:** The wardens of the lower labyrinth remember a time when every corridor ended in a locked gate. They now carve their own signs into the stone, choosing who may enter and what may leave. A covenant with the polis is judged by conduct, not flattery. Those who march above ground carry their weight without haste, stubborn enough to remain when lighter troops begin to look for a road behind them. Their loyalty is an agreement, not a property of their horns.

**Upgrade lore:** A Labyrinth Warden is entrusted with the passages others must not lose. Its steadiness is represented by the minimum-morale rule; it does not make the whole army fearless.

## Cyclops / Stormforged Cyclops

**Mythology note:** Hesiod's *Theogony* associates named Cyclopes with Zeus' thunder weapon, while *Odyssey* 9 presents Polyphemus in a different narrative setting. Sources: [L04](../reference/01_sources_and_version_scope.md). The game's siege specialist combines selected motifs as original fiction; it should not collapse these texts into one supposedly uniform tradition.

**Game lore:** Thunder Quarry is less a mine than a negotiation with the mountain. Its keepers know which seams can be opened and which should be left beneath the storm. A Cyclops may spend a day shaping a single stone until it sits correctly in one vast hand. When the polis asks for aid, the bargain includes the work afterward: roads repaired, fallen walls cleared, and a quarry made safe again. In battle, the same patience becomes a frightening economy of movement.

**Upgrade lore:** Stormforged Cyclopes carry stones marked by the forge's rites. The name does not add lightning area damage unless a future rule explicitly introduces it.
